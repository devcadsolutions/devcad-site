From drafting plant models to writing the tools

My first job was modelling incinerator plants and producing detail drawings for Kawasaki projects in Japan. The work was precise, repetitive, and full of steps a script could do better, so I started writing VBA, AutoLISP and PML to handle them.

That turned into a career. I’ve built tool palettes, installers and standards tooling for two Australian engineering firms, and now Revit, AutoCAD and Autodesk Platform Services tools for a landscape architecture practice.

Through DevCAD Solutions I take on the same kind of work for other teams: the add-in, script or cloud workflow that takes a weekly chore off people’s desks.

How I approach the work

  • Remove the repeat

    I look for the step people do by hand every week, and the check that depends on someone remembering it.

  • Fit the drawing workflow

    Tools are built around sheet sets, linked models, exports and office standards, not around generic software ideas.

  • Make it easy to adopt

    A tool nobody trusts doesn’t get used. I think about the ribbon button, the error message and the first run.

  • Leave it documented

    Guides and handover notes so your team can use and maintain the tool after I’ve moved on.
